About

The throughline

A geneticist and scientist-policy professional working across frontier AI, biotechnology, and national security.

Most safety work looks inward at individual systems. My argument is that the real risk (and the real opportunity) sits at the edges, in how systems, people, and information interact.

I currently serve as the Principal Scientific Advisor at Computercraft, and am affiliated with the Center for a New American Security and the International Computer Science Institute at Berkeley.

My research interests are grounded in a framework I call relational biosecurity (published in Frontiers in Microbiology) which argues that emergent capability and risk in AI-enabled biology live in compositional interactions between systems, not within any single safeguard. I write about the same ideas, less formally, at Support Life.
